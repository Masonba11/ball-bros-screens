Why Solar Screens?

Make Your Home More Comfortable

Solar screens are one of the most effective ways to manage Arizona heat — and they work on every window in your home.

Reduce Heat Entering Your Home

Solar screens block a significant portion of solar heat before it passes through your glass, helping to keep rooms cooler.

Block Harsh Arizona Sun

Arizona's intense UV exposure can be relentless. Solar screens are designed to absorb and reflect sunlight before it hits your windows.

Reduce Glare

Glare on TVs, monitors, and furniture is a daily frustration in the Valley. Solar screens dramatically cut glare without darkening your rooms.

Increase Daytime Privacy

See out clearly while making it difficult for neighbors or passersby to see into your home during the day.

Protect Flooring & Furniture

UV rays fade carpets, hardwood floors, furniture, and artwork over time. Solar screens help shield your interiors from sun damage.

Help Your AC Work Less Hard

By reducing solar heat gain through windows, solar screens can help reduce the load on your air conditioning system.

Improve Curb Appeal

Modern solar screens have a clean, professional look that complements your home's exterior and gives it a finished appearance.

Custom Fit for Every Window

Every screen is measured and built specifically for your windows — no gaps, no sag, no one-size-fits-all shortcuts.

The Arizona Problem

Arizona Sun Is Hard on Your Home — and Your AC

If you live in the East Valley, you know what direct afternoon sun does to certain rooms. Temperatures spike, glare makes screens unwatchable, and your air conditioning runs harder to keep up. Some rooms may become unusable during peak afternoon hours.

The problem isn't just outside — it's pouring through your windows every day. Standard window screens do nothing to block heat or UV. And window tint or blackout curtains mean giving up your view and natural light.

Solar screens offer a better solution. They're installed on the outside of your windows — blocking heat and UV before it ever reaches the glass — while still letting you see out and keeping your home bright.
